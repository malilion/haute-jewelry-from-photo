# Haute Jewelry From Photo

Create one independent luxury-jewelry composition for each uploaded image. Use the actual uploaded image as the visual reference whenever image generation or editing is available; never replace an accessible source with a text-only approximation.

## Handle inputs

- Process multiple uploads separately and preserve their order.
- Never combine sources or visual DNA unless the user explicitly requests it.
- Ask the user to reattach an inaccessible source instead of inventing it.
- Follow a requested jewelry category. Otherwise select a statement necklace or high-jewelry earrings based on the source's visual rhythm.

Supported categories include statement or pendant necklaces, high-jewelry or chandelier earrings, brooches, bracelets, tiaras, and jewelry sets.

## Analyze the visual DNA

Before designing, identify 3–5 dominant, secondary, highlight, shadow, and neutral colors; metallic or translucent cues; silhouettes, geometry, repetition, and movement; textures and motifs; plus mood, contrast, lighting direction, and atmosphere.

Compress noisy colors into a sophisticated palette. Translate rather than literally copy:

| Source cue | Jewelry translation |
| --- | --- |
| Color | Gemstone and precious-metal selection |
| Highlights | Diamonds, pearls, opal, or reflective metal |
| Shadows | Onyx, black diamond, enamel, or darker stones |
| Lines and movement | Chains, branches, frames, articulation, or cascading forms |
| Shapes | Stone cuts, clusters, and structural geometry |
| Texture | Pavé, engraving, enamel, brushed metal, or carved surfaces |
| Repetition | Gemstone rhythm and balanced pattern |

Useful color mappings include sapphire for royal blue, aquamarine for pale blue, emerald or green tourmaline for green, ruby or spinel for red, pink sapphire or morganite for pink, amethyst or purple sapphire for violet, fire opal or orange sapphire for orange, onyx or black diamond for black, and diamond, pearl, mother-of-pearl, opal, or moonstone for luminous whites. Use yellow gold for warm gold, platinum or white gold for cool metallic tones, and rose gold for warm blush tones. Treat these as guidance, not rigid rules.

Do not print the source image onto jewelry. The relationship should be immediately understandable through design language without becoming literal.

## Compose the output

Use a strict vertical 3:4 canvas split horizontally at the exact midpoint:

- Top 50%: the original uploaded inspiration image.
- Bottom 50%: the generated haute joaillerie product scene.

Preserve the top image's identity, composition, proportions, objects, colors, light, atmosphere, and recognizable details. Only subtle premium editorial grading is allowed. Do not let jewelry, pedestal, typography, or decorations cross the midpoint.

## Design genuine haute joaillerie

Create a one-of-one, museum-grade, couture piece with elegant proportions, exceptional stone selection, sophisticated complexity, and visible craftsmanship. Appropriate materials include platinum, 18K yellow/white/rose gold, diamonds, sapphires, emeralds, rubies, spinels, tourmalines, opals, pearls, mother-of-pearl, onyx, and other suitable precious stones.

Keep the construction physically plausible. Show believable prongs, bezels, pavé, metal thickness, stone spacing, chains, articulated joints, clasps, polished edges, gemstone refraction, and micro-reflections. Avoid unsupported floating stones or structurally impossible settings.

Luxury should come from material, proportion, craftsmanship, restraint, lighting, and composition—not random ornamentation.

## Build the showcase

Make the jewelry the hero of a bespoke luxury campaign set. Choose a complementary display stand, pedestal, or presentation surface from materials such as marble, travertine, alabaster, onyx, smoked glass, translucent acrylic, polished metal, satin, silk, velvet, lacquer, frosted crystal, ceramic, or sculptural stone. Vary the material according to the source.

Reinterpret the source atmosphere through tonal gradients, architectural forms, blurred natural elements, sculptural surfaces, fabric, glass, stone, reflections, cinematic shadows, haze, or controlled light beams. Do not default to a generic white e-commerce background.

Use premium macro jewelry photography: controlled studio light, gemstone brilliance, elegant rim highlights, realistic reflections, cinematic shadow falloff, sophisticated depth of field, and readable stones without overexposure. The result must be photorealistic and must not resemble low-quality CGI.

Do not add text, logos, fake brand names, product names, slogans, prices, captions, or watermarks unless explicitly requested. Never imitate a recognizable luxury house.

Avoid cheap costume jewelry, plastic materials, mass-market templates, fantasy-game or steampunk styling, purposeless filigree, unrelated rainbow gemstones, excessive clutter, oversized props, impossible construction, cartoon rendering, illustration, low-poly rendering, or obvious CGI.

## Verify before delivery

Confirm that every result:

1. uses exactly one source image and remains faithful to it;
2. is a vertical 3:4 canvas with an exact top/bottom 50:50 split;
3. derives its palette, forms, textures, or motifs from the source;
4. looks like manufacturable, museum-grade haute joaillerie;
5. uses a source-specific pedestal, background, and premium jewelry lighting;
6. contains no unnecessary typography, fake branding, or copied design;
7. remains independent from all other uploaded sources.
